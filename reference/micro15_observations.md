# Micro 15, Satu Mare: what the reference shows

Sources: `satellite/micro15_z18.png` (Esri World Imagery at zoom 18, about 0.40 m per pixel, summer with full leaf; zoom 19 isn't available here), the photos in `photos/`, and the street names in the existing `micro15_osm.json`. Positions are approximate (±10 m).

Rough pixel-to-coordinate conversion for `micro15_z18.png` (1792 x 2048 px):
lat ≈ 47.78548 − y·3.605e-6, lon ≈ 22.85706 + x·5.364e-6. North is up. Shadows fall to the north-west, so the long dark bands north of each block show how tall it is.

## 1. Layout and landmarks

- **Strada Careiului** runs west to east at about lat 47.7840, about 400 px from the top of the image. It's the wide main road: 2+2 lanes of dark asphalt, white dashed lane lines, and zebra crossings at about lon 22.8596 and at the roundabout. The blocks along its north side have parking lots between them and the road. On the south side there's a continuous belt of big mature trees.
- **Careiului × Bulevardul Lalelei roundabout** is at about 47.7841, 22.8652. It has a small planted central island. The zebra crossings on its four arms are painted on **red/pink asphalt panels**, which show clearly from above.
- **Lalelei / Strada Prahova** leaves that roundabout heading SSW in a long curve (about 22.865 → 22.862) and ends at a second roundabout. It's 2+2 lanes with parked cars along the west side and a thick strip of trees on the east side.
- **Dariu Pop × Oituz × Inului roundabout** is at about 47.7794, 22.8615. **Strada Oituz** goes west from it (2 lanes, cars parked half on the kerb). **Strada Dariu Pop** goes ESE and is the southern edge of the block district. South of Dariu Pop it's all **private houses with orange/red clay-tile roofs**, gardens and fruit trees.
- **Spitalul Județean (county hospital)**: a large dark-roofed complex centred on 47.7830, 22.8630, with a grass lawn and a painted helipad circle with an "H" at about 47.7828, 22.8640.
- **Piața Someș market** (47.7818–47.7823, 22.8641–22.8656): a large roof in blue and grey sections.
- **School Bălcescu-Petőfi** (about 47.7817, 22.8577) has an orange tile roof and a red hard court. There's a **green artificial-turf pitch** next to it at about 47.7820, 22.8584.
- **Kindergarten nr. 13** (47.7807–47.7811, 22.8599–22.8605) has orange tile hip roofs and a grassy play yard with bright play equipment.
- **Reformed church "15"** (about 47.7799, 22.8611) has a steep orange roof and stands just NW of the Dariu Pop roundabout.
- A dark-green fenced **sports pitch** at about 47.7813, 22.8611.
- **Shopping City mall** and **Lidl** sit east of Lalelei, north-east of the area, under large flat white roofs.

## 2. Blocks of flats

- There are two main types:
  1. **P+4 slab blocks**, about 60–90 m long and 11–12 m deep. Most run north–south in the eastern part (between Prahova and Neajlov, 47.7805–47.7815, 22.864–22.867). West of Prahova and along Oituz they're rotated about 30° (NW–SE).
  2. **P+8/P+10 point towers**, roughly square at 20–25 m a side. There's a row of four or five along Aleea Milcov / Aleea Ozana at about lat 47.7813–47.7816, lon 22.858–22.861. Their long shadows confirm the height. There are also tall slabs along Careiului: the photos show P+10 there.
- **Roofs:** they're flat everywhere. Most are light grey bitumen or gravel, with darker stairwell and lift-machine boxes poking up at regular intervals, one per entrance (scara). Many roofs are **re-done in patches, one scara at a time**: the bright **red** and **blue** sections on the N–S slabs at about 47.7808–47.7815, lon 22.8656 and 22.8662 are easy to see. A game recreation should give each stairwell section its own roof colour or wear.
- **Facades** (from the boulevard photos): prefab concrete panels in beige or grey, some repainted in pastels (cream, pale yellow, salmon). Thermal insulation (polystyrene) is applied **per flat**, so you get a patchwork of freshly plastered rectangles next to old grey panels. The balconies are mostly **closed in by the residents**, each in a different way: white PVC, older brown or aluminium frames, some still open with railings. Satellite dishes, AC units and drying laundry hang on them.
- Ground floors along Careiului and Prahova have **shops built into the blocks** (pharmacy, Profi, Superbet, kebab, opticians).

## 3. Yards between the blocks

- The yards are **heavily treed**. Large deciduous trees with crowns about 10–15 m across, about as tall as a P+4 block, cover much of each courtyard. Typical species for the area are lime/linden, chestnut, poplar and birch. There are also a few dark **conifers** (spruce or pine), and from the photos, trimmed shrubs and hedges along the verges. The biggest masses of trees are south of Careiului (22.857–22.862) and around the hospital.
- **Worn dirt paths**: beige desire lines cut diagonally across the lawns between block entrances and the shops, especially around 47.7811–47.7814, 22.8576–22.8582 and in the yards west of the hospital. Lawns are patchy with bare earth under the trees.
- **Parking** takes up nearly every space alongside the blocks: asphalt bays at right angles, often a full row along the long side of a block. It's very dense near Careiului (lots at 47.7824–47.7830, 22.8604–22.8609 with about 60 cars) and along Dariu Pop. Some areas are just **bare gravel or dirt** used for parking, for example around 47.7838, 22.857–22.858.
- **Garages**: long rows of single-car garages with flat **rust-brown or reddish metal roofs**, about 5–6 m deep:
  - an E–W row at about 47.7810, 22.8595–22.8610, with a short N–S leg near 22.8608;
  - grey flat-roofed rows east of Lalelei at about 47.7818–47.7825, 22.8667–22.8672.
- **Playgrounds and hard courts**: small sandy or beige squares with equipment, and a small grey asphalt square (an old basketball or carpet-beating area) at about 47.7812, 22.8583. There's a sandy play plaza next to a parking lot at about 47.7809, 22.8640, and bright red and pink equipment or benches at about 47.7801, 22.8635. Playgrounds here tend to be small and sit between two blocks rather than in the middle of the yard.
- **Kiosks and sheds**: small white or tin-roofed boxes 3–5 m across beside parking lots, for example about 47.7809, 22.8636. There are also red-roofed sheds near the yards south of Careiului (about 47.7838, 22.8588).
- **Leftover houses**: a few pre-block houses with tile roofs and their own fenced gardens survive *inside* the block district (about 47.7798–47.7801, 22.8642–22.8653). These are fenced with concrete or metal fences (see `street_old_houses_fence.jpg`).

## 4. Roads and street furniture

- Main roads are dark asphalt with white dashed lane lines and zebra crossings, some on red panels. Secondary streets (Oituz, Dariu Pop, Tisa) have two lanes, lighter and **patched asphalt**, and often no centre line. Kerbs are concrete. There are grass verges with trees between the road and the pavement (asphalt or concrete slabs).
- Service roads inside the yards are narrow (about 4–5 m), with cracked asphalt or gravel and cars parked along them.
- Photos of the boulevards (2010s) still show the **trolleybus overhead wires** on concrete and steel poles across the road. Satu Mare ran trolleybuses until 2005. This is a good period detail for a childhood-era version. Street lamps are tall single-arm poles. Look out for concrete flower planters, STOP signs, blue pedestrian-crossing signs, and yellow **Ikarus buses** (`city_street_ikarus_bus.jpg`).

## 5. Things a recreation should get right

1. Wide, straight Careiului with tall blocks, shops on the ground floor and parking in front, contrasting with the leafy, quieter yards behind them.
2. Trees far outnumber open lawn inside the yards. The canopy shades most paths.
3. Every block is a patchwork: stairwell sections with differently coloured roofs, flat-by-flat insulation, and mismatched enclosed balconies.
4. Cars and garages are everywhere: rows of rusty garages, gravel lots, cars parked on the kerbs.
5. Small playgrounds with sand and metal equipment, dirt shortcut paths, and kiosks by the parking lots.
6. Red-tiled private houses start right at Dariu Pop to the south, and a few survive inside the district.
7. Landmarks for orientation: the hospital with its helipad lawn, the two roundabouts (Careiului/Lalelei and Dariu Pop/Oituz), the market roof, and the reformed church.

Limits: the satellite image is a single summer shot from above, so facade colours come from the boulevard photos, not from the yards. Tree species are inferred, not confirmed. I didn't find a Commons photo of the inside of a Micro 15 yard. Street View, or Johnny's own memory, would be the next source for yard-level detail.
